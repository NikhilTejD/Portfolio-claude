Rescuing a stalled WhatsApp governance rollout

Mana Mitra moved government services onto WhatsApp. In my three secretariats, adoption had flatlined at 12%. I found out why, redesigned the rollout, and took it to 31%.

My roleRollout supervisor
Scope3 secretariats · ~10,000 citizens
DurationJun 2025 – ongoing
Result12% → 31% adoption
Context Diagnosis Approach Results Reflection Artifacts

Context

The state launched Mana Mitra so citizens could request certificates and services over WhatsApp instead of visiting a secretariat counter. I supervised the rollout for three secretariats in Madakasira mandal, Sri Sathya Sai District.

The first push followed the standard playbook: awareness banners, trained local volunteers to guide neighbours, and operators nudging citizens to switch. Adoption barely moved, and administrators wanted higher numbers fast.

Diagnosis

Instead of running more campaigns, I spoke directly with different citizen groups to find out what was stopping them. Mapped against ADKAR, the rollout wasn't failing at awareness. It was breaking at Desire and Ability.

AwarenessCampaigns reached people
KnowledgeVolunteers explained the steps
DesireBlocked: fear and distrust
AbilityBlocked: low digital literacy
ReinforcementNeeded once usage began
Fear of costly mistakesAn error in self-service might cost a benefit or mean extra office visits to fix.
Trust in operatorsPeople trusted a familiar operator more than a phone screen.
An ageing populationYounger residents had migrated to Bengaluru for work, leaving fewer people comfortable with smartphones.
Distrust of soft copiesDigital documents felt less valid than stamped hard copies.

Approach

I proposed a redesigned rollout and got administrator buy-in. The idea was to make self-service the easier path while keeping the most vulnerable citizens protected.

  1. Jun 2025

    Organic rollout

    Banners, volunteers and operator nudges. Adoption stalled at 12%.

  2. 2025

    Ground diagnosis

    Conversations across citizen segments; resistance mapped to Desire and Ability.

  3. 2025

    Redesigned rollout

    Reduced the supply of physical forms used for offline service, reserved remaining counter capacity for elderly citizens, and set up trainer-staffed stalls beside operators so people could try self-service with help on hand.

  4. May 2026

    Support capacity lost

    Volunteers and operators were reassigned to Census and then SIR work, cutting the help desks that made the new model work.

  5. Ongoing

    Keep improving

    Awareness work continues alongside new tweaks to lift adoption further.

Results

Organic rollout
12%
After redesign
31%

Share of citizens using Mana Mitra for services, across 3 secretariats. Scale runs 0–100%.

Adoption more than doubled. Supervisors in other locations picked up the same stall model on their own, which told me the fix was transferable rather than local luck.

Reflection

Restricting the offline route worked, but it created friction for citizens. With more time and support capacity, I would lead with the trainer stalls and phase down offline supply more gradually, so adoption feels chosen rather than pushed.
